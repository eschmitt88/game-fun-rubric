# information-gap-curiosity

## Definition

Curiosity arises when attention focuses on a perceived, closable gap in knowledge; tolerance for the gap depends on confidence in closing it, not on its size (Loewenstein 1994).

## Why it matters here

- **to2016integrating** — Mechanism for 6.3; explains why failure cost and feedback, not raw difficulty, decide whether uncertainty is fun.
- **tang2025designing** — N=19 qualitative study of treasure-chest curiosity (six types incl. novel Future Rewards Maximization Curiosity); CCA/PC gaps decay within ~3-5 exposures to a static reward pool, and dense/mismatched-reward chest frequency independently produces a named "discovery fatigue" via diminishing marginal utility and habituation — direct evidence for 6.1's pacing anchors and 6.5's player-explored-vs-system-awarded distinction.
- **kao2024how** — Pre-registered SEM, N=1,699: curiosity is the strongest predictor of enjoyment and the *only* significant predictor of voluntary playtime (both models, p<.001), stronger than competence or effectance. Sharpens Loewenstein's "closable gap" account: *reducible/epistemic* uncertainty (success-dependent hit-or-miss feedback) recruited curiosity; *irreducible/aleatoric* randomness (stochastic variable feedback) did not — not all "unexpected variety" is equally curiosity-inducing.
- **tang2024exploring** — N=482 survey, the quantitative foundation for tang2025designing's typology: seven curiosity categories (Perceptual, Manipulatory, Complexity/Ambiguity, Epistemic, Adjustive-Reactive, Social, Future Rewards Maximization) split into 13 questionnaire dimensions. EFA+CFA (CFI=.928, RMSEA=.061) found the **13 dimensions behave as largely independent factors, not as instances of the 7 parent categories** — direct evidence against treating dimension 6's five criteria as proxies for one underlying curiosity construct. Introduces **Future Rewards Maximization Curiosity** (Dubey & Griffiths 2020): curiosity as choosing actions to maximize *future* reward under incomplete information — the same "solver's uncertainty" mechanism G2/5.2 already model, reached independently via a curiosity rather than a decision-theory framing.
- **yow2024thrill** — Cross-sectional N=398 survey, 5DCR trait-curiosity taxonomy (six dispositional dimensions incl. joyous exploration, thrill seeking, deprivation sensitivity, social curiosity). **Not a replication of kao2024how, and a mild complication rather than corroboration**: joyous exploration — the 5DCR facet closest to this concept's info-gap construct — did **not** significantly predict self-reported playtime (β=−.04, p=.491); the one significant playtime predictor was thrill seeking (arousal/risk-seeking, R²=.02 total), a dimension that doesn't map onto information-gap curiosity at all. Different curiosity operationalization (trait/dispositional vs. kao2024how's state/manipulated in-game construct), different DV (retrospective aggregate recall vs. objective free-choice minutes), near-null effect size. Joyous exploration *did* predict enjoyment of reward-and-punishment game features (B=.08, p=.034) — a distinct, weaker claim. Read as adjacent evidence, not as satisfying ADR 0006's "replicated in a second game" dimension-6 reweighting trigger.
- **gomezmaureira2021level** — Bayesian factorial, N=254, second independent game (*Shinobi Valley*, open-world exploration, no combat): curiosity-inducing **level design patterns** (Extreme Points, Out-of-Place Elements, Spatial Connections, Visual Obstructions) causally increased *behavioural* exploration (game-metric distance/entropy, decisive Bayes factors), with Out-of-Place Elements and Extreme Points outperforming Visual Obstructions (frequently misread as an environmental boundary rather than an invitation — a legibility problem, not a motivation problem). Trait curiosity (5DC) did **not** predict exploratory behaviour (H3 rejected) — convergent with yow2024thrill's mixed trait-curiosity picture, though a different trait instrument (5DC vs 5DCR) and a different DV (logged behaviour vs self-reported playtime). **Also not a replication of kao2024how**: tests design-pattern→behaviour causation, not curiosity-as-competing-mediator of self-reported enjoyment/playtime, and measures no competence/effectance construct to adjudicate against — so it does not trigger ADR 0006's reweighting bar either, but it is the strongest *behavioural* (not self-report) evidence yet that curiosity-oriented design moves player action. New: an explicit goal statement measurably *defers* pattern-driven exploration until the goal is met, then exploration resumes — a goal/curiosity crowding-out dynamic not previously in this concept.
