# accessibility-as-gate-on-joy

## Definition

Accessibility barriers function as a *gate*, not a subtractor: below some
player-relative threshold of ability mismatch ("access difficulty" —
inaccessibility understood through the lens of difficulty, existing
between a player and a game rather than as a fixed property of the game),
a player cannot reach a game's designed challenge or joy at all, no matter
how well-designed that challenge is once reached. Above the threshold, the
same ability mismatch can itself become a desirable "hard mode" —
deliberately sought-out struggle, fiero, and social bonding (access
intimacy) — via the practice of **disabled gaming**: expert, creative
navigation of access difficulty (deviant play, hacking, metagaming), not
merely deficit-repair. The two outcomes look identical from the outside
(a player struggling against an ability mismatch) and are told apart only
by whether the mismatch is small enough to cross.

## Why it matters here

- **martinez2024playing** — Interview study (N=13 gamers with
  disabilities, reflexive thematic analysis) source for the whole concept.
  Concrete gate/no-gate contrast: Aaron (legally blind) derives real fiero
  from beating nondisabled friends despite disadvantage in *Battlefield*;
  Eddy streams his access hacks as a point of pride; Caleb simply could
  not clear a wall-jump needing more simultaneous inputs than he could
  manage, and abandoned the game — same "hard mode" framing, opposite
  outcome, because his mismatch didn't cross the gate. The paper's *Mario
  Kart* driving-assistant thought experiment shows the gate cuts both
  ways: an "accessibility" fix that auto-solves the ability mismatch can
  delete the exact challenge a disabled player was there for (removes
  Dylan/Mira's exploration joy, Aaron's hard-mode victory, Blue's access
  intimacy) — consequence-based accessibility (a personal
  benefit-vs-consequence calculation, not a binary judgment) is how
  players themselves navigate this trade-off.

## Connections

- [[intuitive-controls-price-of-admission]] — sibling "gate/subtractor"
  framing for Dimension 8 (usability), but that concept is *universal*
  (intuitive controls help everyone at the margin); this concept is
  *player-relative* (the same access difficulty can be absent, a
  desirable hard mode, or a hard gate depending on the individual
  player's abilities) — a categorically different kind of gate from G1/G2,
  which are universal pass/fail, not player-relative.
- [[failure-and-difficulty]] — access difficulty is difficulty in
  Salen & Zimmerman's "artificial conflict" sense, but sourced from a
  player-game ability mismatch rather than designed challenge escalation;
  shares the same "not all difficulty is desirable" caution.
- [[multidimensional-challenge-taxonomy]] — access difficulty is a further,
  player-relative axis of challenge orthogonal to Denisova et al.'s
  cognitive/emotional/performative/decision-making split; worth checking
  whether disabled participants' accounts map cleanly onto that taxonomy
  or need their own category.
- [[player-experience-measurement]] — this concept's evidence is
  qualitative/interview-based (E3), not psychometric; flags a measurement
  gap (no validated accessibility-satisfaction instrument analogous to
  PENS/PXI/BANGS exists in this project's graph).
- [[design-evidence-quality]] — anchors the E3 tier for this concept's
  claims (grounded qualitative theory from a single primary study, not yet
  independently corroborated or quantified).
