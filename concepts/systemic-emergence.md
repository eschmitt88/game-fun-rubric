# systemic-emergence

## Definition

Unscripted outcomes arising from mechanics interacting with each other and
with player input over time, rather than being authored directly. Juul's
sharper formulation: "a small number of rules combine and yield large
numbers of game variations, which the players then design strategies for
dealing with" (juul2002open) — split into three subtypes of ascending
strength: **rule interaction** (simple, directly-derivable, unanticipated
consequences — e.g. rocket-jumping; "not really emergence"), **combination**
(breadth of possible states/sessions the rules allow), and **emergent
strategies** (properties not immediately deductible from the rules —
teamplay, dominant lines). Emergence is *weak* emergence (Bedau) — derivable
from the rules via simulation, not ontologically novel — and is explicitly
not the same as "the player is free": an emergent system still converges on
regular session-level patterns (Counter-Strike sessions "almost always lead
to fights," Monopoly games "always end with a player going bankrupt")
because players pursue the game's goal and search for a good strategy. Juul
frames this as a **third way** between a fully designer-specified ("closed")
game and total player freedom ("open") — emergence is neither.

## Why it matters here

- **hunicke2004mda** — MDA's thesis: dynamics (emergent) produce aesthetics.
- **juul2002open** — Names and defines emergence itself; gives it a
  three-way taxonomy (rule interaction / combination / emergent strategies)
  and the open↔closed spectrum framing; the walkthrough-vs-strategy-guide
  heuristic is a cheap proxy for scoring how emergent a game is. Primary
  source for rubric criterion 6.2 (previously anchored only to
  hunicke2004mda's passing mention); also feeds 1.1 (depth/breadth of
  pattern space — "large numbers of game variations" from few rules) and,
  more weakly, 2.6 (distinctiveness: "EverQuest has a distinct feel to it"
  attributed to emergent rule structure, not authored content).
