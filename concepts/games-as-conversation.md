# games-as-conversation

## Definition

Gameplay read as a cooperative communicative exchange between player and
game: player and game actions are locutionary speech acts (Austin/Searle)
performed in service of illocutionary goals (assertive, directive,
commissive, expressive, declarative), and both sides are held — loosely,
since the game has no real agency to consent — to a Gricean Cooperative
Contract of four maxims: **Quantity** (not too much/little information),
**Quality** (no untrue options for action), **Relation** (contextually
relevant), and **Manner** (unobfuscated). A maxim can be deliberately
*flaunted* for effect (Skyrim's cliff-edge discovery notification inviting
a jump) as distinct from *violated* by design failure (E.T.'s illegible
feedback).

## Why it matters here

- **cardonarivera2014games** — Source of the whole framing. Directly
  strengthens three rubric criteria that currently lack a formal
  theoretical anchor: **4.4 state legibility** (Maxim of Manner is a
  linguistics-sourced formalization of "legible"), **8.3 rules are
  learnable** (the rule system as a legal grammar of locutionary acts;
  Maxim of Quality maps to "no untrue options for action"), and **8.6
  expectation calibration** (Maxim of Relation, flaunted vs. violated, is
  a precise model of "designed vs. accidental surprise"). Also offers a
  complementary, independent lens on **2.6 distinctive, coherent agency**
  (nguyen2019games) — agency as a *speech-act vocabulary* rather than
  purely an aesthetic medium. Reframes the "narrative paradox"/boundary
  problem (how much player freedom before coherence breaks) as a
  discourse-planning problem, distinguishing exogenous (social, personal,
  utility) from endogenous (formal/motivational, narrative) determinants
  of player choice.
