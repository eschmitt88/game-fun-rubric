# Rater statements (v0.6)

Rate agreement 0–4 with each statement, or **N/A** when the statement
cannot apply to this game (record why). Statements aggregate by mean to
their parent rubric row (e.g. 3.1 = mean of 3.1a–3.1c); rows aggregate per
`docs/rubric.md` v0.5 (weights, gates, subtotals, cross-reads). The
referent is always *the game as its target player (S1) experiences it* —
not the designer's intent (that is scored in the separate S2 pass) and not
your own taste.

## Gates

- **G1a.** With art, story, meta-progression and juice stripped or toggled off, the 30-second core loop is still worth repeating.
- **G2a.** The game contains a recurring choice whose best answer is neither obvious nor a blind guess.
- **G2b.** That choice has stakes the target player cares about.

## 1 Learning & mastery

- **1.1a.** New patterns, tools or situations that demand new learning keep appearing until the end of the runtime.
- **1.1b.** The game exercises at least two distinct challenge types (cognitive, performative, emotional, decision-making).
- **1.1c.** Known strategies have counter-situations that punish rote repetition.
- **1.2a.** Skills learned early are prerequisites that unlock visibly new abilities or situations later.
- **1.2b.** Mastering one system makes sibling systems faster to learn.
- **1.3a.** After a failure, the player can say specifically what to do differently next time.
- **1.3b.** The game shows progress toward mastery clearly enough that improvement is felt without checking menus.
- **1.3c.** Post-play review of what happened (replays, run summaries) is available. *(N/A for games with no run structure)*
- **1.4a.** A spectator could tell a skilled player from a novice within a minute of watching.
- **1.4b.** The strongest feedback moments are attached to the actions that define skill in this game.
- **1.5a.** There is no single build, route or strategy that makes other options pointless.

## 2 Agency & meaningful choice

- **2.1a.** Interesting decisions occur inside the core loop itself, not only in pause menus.
- **2.1b.** Making those decisions does not interrupt the flow of play.
- **2.2a.** Choices are trade-offs between goods, not puzzles with one right answer.
- **2.2b.** Players argue with each other about the best build, route or approach. *(N/A pre-release: substitute playtest disagreement)*
- **2.3a.** The consequences of earlier choices remain visible and traceable later.
- **2.3b.** The game gives enough foresight that an early, persistent choice cannot silently ruin a run.
- **2.4a.** The same obstacle can be beaten in more than one valid way.
- **2.4b.** Approaches the designers did not plan for still work. *(Optional/post-game routes count when aimed at the target player)*
- **2.5a.** The target player can set and pursue goals of their own inside the structure.
- **2.5b.** The player is never forced through long stretches of actions they did not choose.
- **2.6a.** The game's combination of goals, abilities and constraints feels like a specific way of being, recognisably different from other games.
- **2.6b.** Players describe the game by what it lets them do, not by its genre label.

## 3 Challenge–skill balance & flow

- **3.1a.** Difficulty rises and falls in waves, guaranteeing both failures and successes on the way up.
- **3.1b.** The player can adjust effective difficulty from inside play (which fight, depth or route to take), not only from a settings menu.
- **3.1c.** Each challenge type the game uses is separately well-calibrated. *(N/A if only one type)*
- **3.1d.** Opposition is neither trivially weak nor illegitimately advantaged.
- **3.2a.** Losing costs enough to matter but little enough that the player wants to retry immediately.
- **3.2b.** Failure never forces long mechanical replay of already-mastered content.
- **3.2c.** The player is never left with no way to finish (hopelessness).
- **3.3a.** Inputs map so reliably to outcomes that players blame themselves for failure — and are right to.
- **3.3b.** Random events resolve after the player's decision (affecting outcomes), not before it (invalidating plans). *(N/A if no randomness)*
- **3.4a.** Play is continuous: interruptions are player-initiated, not imposed.
- **3.4b.** The workload is high but within the player's perceptual and cognitive limits.
- **3.4c.** No task the game demands feels unimportant.
- **3.5a.** At the end of a loop there is always a concrete reason to play one more.
- **3.5b.** The game also offers natural stopping points where quitting feels clean.
- **3.5c.** The pull to continue coexists with high autonomy/competence satisfaction — it does not substitute for it. *(the ADR 0005 compulsion check)*

## 4 Feel & feedback

- **4.1a.** Controls respond within the timing the game's actions demand (latency, buffering, cancel windows).
- **4.2a.** Every input is acknowledged instantly, at minimum with sound.
- **4.2b.** The state change caused by an action is readable before any embellishment plays.
- **4.2c.** Feedback strength scales with actual accomplishment, not with mere activity.
- **4.2d.** Layered juice stops short of obscuring what the player caused. *(the outcome-binding ceiling)*
- **4.3a.** Objects move with believable weight and momentum.
- **4.3b.** The core movement or manipulation is pleasant with no goal at all.
- **4.4a.** Health, resources and threats are readable at a glance during action.
- **4.4b.** Detail is inspectable on demand without leaving play.
- **4.5a.** The game has music, not only sound effects.
- **4.5b.** Every effect is consistent with the world's physics and art style.
- **4.5c.** Interface chrome is themed to the world.
- **4.5d.** Sounds and voice lines are varied enough not to grate over a session.

## 5 Goals, progression & pacing

- **5.1a.** At any moment the player can name a short-, a medium- and a long-term goal, all running simultaneously.
- **5.2a.** The outcome of the current arc stays genuinely uncertain until near its end.
- **5.2b.** The game uses at least two distinct uncertainty mechanisms (variable difficulty, multi-level goals, hidden information, randomness).
- **5.3a.** Progression regularly changes how the game is played, not only its numbers. *(Optional/post-game unlocks count for the target player)*
- **5.4a.** Intensity alternates deliberately — tension and release, novelty and consolidation.
- **5.5a.** The game engineers recurring moments of hard-won triumph that players remember.

## 6 Novelty, curiosity & discovery

- **6.1a.** New content or mechanics keep arriving through to the end of the runtime.
- **6.1b.** Discovery rewards are paced below the saturation point where opening one more feels like a chore.
- **6.2a.** Mechanics interact to produce outcomes nobody scripted.
- **6.2b.** Players trade "did you know you can…" stories about the game.
- **6.3a.** The game plants questions the player wants answered.
- **6.3b.** The player believes those gaps are closable with effort. *(confidence, not gap size)*
- **6.4a.** Trying something weird produces an interesting result more often than a punishment.
- **6.5a.** Some worthwhile discoveries exist only for players who go looking.

## 7 Emotion, fantasy & narrative

- **7.1a.** The game lets the player be something they find appealing, and the mechanics deliver that fantasy.
- **7.2a.** The game evokes more than two distinct emotions in a normal playthrough.
- **7.2b.** At least one deliberately designed negative-valence peak (loss, dread, guilt) lands as memorable rather than merely unpleasant. *(N/A for tonally light games — record the choice)*
- **7.3a.** The skill depends on the fantasy and the fantasy depends on the skill (both directions).
- **7.4a.** A player's identity shows in their play — in builds, style or aesthetics.
- **7.5a.** Players report thinking about the game when not playing it. *(measures appreciation, not fun; report separately)*

## 8 Clarity, friction & expectation

- **8.1a.** Teaching is invisible inside play within the first minute.
- **8.1b.** Tutorial investment matches how much the mechanics genuinely need explaining. *(a conventional game with no tutorial can score 4)*
- **8.1c.** The game never forces practice of an already-understood action.
- **8.2a.** Time in menus is a small fraction of session time.
- **8.3a.** A player can build a correct mental model of the rules from playing; surprises come from combination, not hidden information the game withheld unfairly.
- **8.4a.** Recovery from any error is quick — checkpoints and load times do not force replay.
- **8.5a.** Difficulty and control options broaden who can reach the game's designed joy. *(access is a gate: score 0 if the target audience includes players for whom no option makes play possible)*
- **8.6a.** The game signals what kind of challenge, constraint or randomness is coming, so surprises feel designed rather than unfair.
