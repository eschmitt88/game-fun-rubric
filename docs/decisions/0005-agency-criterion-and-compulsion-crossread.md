**Context.** Round 3 sources: nguyen2019games argues the designer's medium
is the whole agency (goals + abilities + constraints), which 2.1–2.5 do not
capture; hopson2001behavioral shows the operant machinery behind 3.5's
"one more" hook is valence-neutral, so 3.5 alone cannot separate a loved
loop from a compulsive one; andersen2012impact (N>45,000) shows tutorial
value is conditional on mechanic discoverability and forced practice never
helps.

**Decision.**
1. New criterion **2.6 Distinctive, coherent agency** (E4, single source —
   revisit if no corroboration by v0.6).
2. **3.5 is read jointly with dimension 2**: strong session pull with low
   autonomy/competence satisfaction (BANGS/PENS) is scored as the compulsion
   signature and does not earn 3.5's top anchor.
3. **8.1 rewritten** as "onboarding scaled to discoverability"; a
   genre-conventional game may score 4 with no tutorial; forced-practice
   patterns and untested on-demand help are 0/1 anchors.
4. Burgun's uncited claims are marked **contested** after zhang2021effect
   reversed one of them.

**Consequences.** The rubric now has 42 scored rows (2 gates + 40); the
worksheet and scorer are updated. Weights are unchanged — no round-3
source supplies relative importance.
