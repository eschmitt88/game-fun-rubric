# Rethinking Carrots — captured summary

Practitioner-facing Gamasutra article by Richard Ryan and Scott Rigby (Immersyve)
introducing the PENS (Player Experience of Need Satisfaction) model to a game-industry
audience and reporting several applied studies of PENS vs. simple "fun"/rating measures
against commercial and engagement outcomes. This is the trade-press companion piece to
the peer-reviewed ryan2006motivational (Motivation and Emotion, 2006); it reports
additional/overlapping applied studies (an 8-month MMO longitudinal study; the Zelda
vs. A Bug's Life comparison; a multi-game HLM lab study) with business framing not in
the academic paper.

## Structure captured

- **Intro / four design goals for a usable player-experience model**: (1) practical,
  fast feedback during development; (2) statistically linked to enjoyment, perceived
  value, recommendation intent, and sustained engagement; (3) empowers designer
  creativity rather than imposing a checklist; (4) a unifying model rather than another
  competing framework.
- **"Sharpening focus" framing**: measuring surface emotions (fun, enjoyment) is like
  cataloguing TV output images; PENS aims at the underlying "three colored lights"
  (competence/autonomy/relatedness) that drive them — a hypothesis-testing model
  layered on top of, not replacing, the SDT/CET base from ryan2006motivational.
- **Competence** ("the need behind our love of challenge"): correlates with enjoyment,
  immersion, perceived value, and commercial variables across genres (Table 1);
  strategy games showed a *weaker* competence-immersion link than action/precision
  genres — read as conceptually sensible (city-management adjustments feel less
  immediately immersive than a precision headshot).
- **Autonomy** ("the need behind our love of freedom"): demotivators named explicitly —
  forced cutscenes, invisible walls, illusory choices. Reported: turn-based strategy
  showed an autonomy-enjoyment correlation of "nearly .50," the largest reported
  autonomy share of any genre in the piece (Table 2); strong autonomy relationships
  also reported for MMOs (WoW), RTS/TBS, and adventure (Zelda). Frames "sequence
  breaking" / speedrun-style rule-bending as autonomy-need behavior.
- **Relatedness** ("the need behind our love of connecting"): named as most salient in
  MMOs and multiplayer FPS (guilds/parties in WoW, corporations in EVE, squads in
  Counter-Strike); flagged as still under active research for strategy games and
  AI-companion contexts (Table 3).
- **Composite PENS** (Table 4): the three needs combined into one score retain
  significant predictive power for player experience and commercial outcomes "across
  nearly all cases," with the *mix* varying by genre (autonomy said to dominate
  sandbox/strategy, competence said to dominate action).
- **"Show me the money" — 8-month MMO longitudinal study** (Table 5/6): PENS measures
  out-predicted simple enjoyment/fun ratings for continuing players' enthusiasm and
  perceived value; in head-to-head regression, enjoyment items lost predictive power
  once PENS was entered. Headline commercial estimate (Immersyve's own framing, not an
  independently audited figure): PENS-informed design could plausibly lift MMO
  retention 15-20%, translating at a $15/month subscription to roughly $25K/month
  incremental revenue per 10,000 subscribers, or ~$3M/year at 100,000 subscribers.
- **Zelda: Ocarina of Time vs. A Bug's Life comparison** (Table 7/8): this is the same
  Study 2 design as ryan2006motivational (GameRankings scores 98% vs. 57%), reported
  here with a trade-press framing — "PENS showed roughly twice the predictive power of
  game [critic] ratings" for enjoyment/perceived value, and critic ratings lost
  predictive value once PENS was entered in the same regression.
- **Multi-genre HLM lab study**: matches ryan2006motivational Study 3 (four games,
  within-subjects); notes the sample was general population, not self-selected
  "gamers," and reads that as evidence PENS-satisfying design has broad, not
  niche-gamer-only, appeal.
- **"Context is king" — rewards section**: rewards (loot, unlocks) are framed as
  motivating only insofar as they *specifically* extend competence, autonomy, or
  relatedness — worked examples: a WoW level-40 mount (autonomy via exploration range +
  competence via faster travel) and the Zelda grappling hook (competence via new
  traversal skill + autonomy via new areas reachable). Practical prescription: before
  building a reward, ask which specific need(s) it extends, not whether it's "cool."
- **"Putting PENS to work"**: explicitly flags PENS as new to games at time of writing,
  recommends involving an experienced research methodologist to build valid
  playtest instruments, and mentions in-progress work toward standardized/benchmarkable
  PENS scores across titles.
- **Conclusion**: three contributions claimed — (1) a compact heuristic model usable
  during development, not just post-hoc; (2) measures underlying motivational energy
  independent of surface behavior, avoiding "feature creep" from purely
  behavior-taxonomy approaches; (3) offers vocabulary for "Serious Games" contexts
  where fun is not the primary goal.
