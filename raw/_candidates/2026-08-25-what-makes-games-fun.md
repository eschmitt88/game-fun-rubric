Ranked for a single-player fun rubric. Empirical/validated instruments
first (they decide the weights), then the foundational theories the rubric
dimensions are named after, then practitioner sources. Dropped from the
sweep (verified but redundant): Przybylski 2010 (superseded by Tyack 2020
review + Ryan 2006), Vlambeer "Art of Screenshake" (overlaps Juice talk),
Schell *Book of Lenses* and Swink *Game Feel* landing pages (books, not
fetchable — cite directly), GMT "Rise of the Systemic Game" (Burgun covers
systemic depth in fetchable text).

## 1. Player Enjoyment in Video Games: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of the Effects of Game Design Choices

- url: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2023.2210880
- type: paper
- summary: Caroux & Pujol (2023) meta-analyse design-choice effects on enjoyment and find only music shows a significant pooled effect; difficulty and control mode do not.
- reason: The sharpest evidence-based check on rubric weights — most "obvious" design levers lack pooled support.

## 2. Development and Validation of the Player Experience Inventory (PXI)

- url: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2019.102370
- type: paper
- summary: Vanden Abeele et al. (2020) validate a 30-item, 10-subscale instrument spanning functional (challenge, controls, clarity) and psychosocial (mastery, immersion, meaning, curiosity, autonomy) consequences.
- reason: Subscales map almost one-to-one onto the rubric's dimensions and give a validated measurement to calibrate against.

## 3. Self-Determination Theory in HCI Games Research: Current Uses and Open Questions

- url: https://pure.itu.dk/ws/files/95235949/Tyack_2020_.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: Tyack & Mekler (CHI 2020) review 110 SDT-citing games papers and find need-satisfaction is widely used but rarely engaged beyond description.
- reason: Tells us where SDT-based "fun" claims are solid vs. superficial before the rubric leans on them.

## 4. The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self-Determination Theory Approach

- url: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: Four studies show perceived autonomy, competence and relatedness independently predict enjoyment, preference and post-play well-being; origin of PENS.
- reason: Primary source for the agency and mastery dimensions.

## 5. GameFlow: A Model for Evaluating Player Enjoyment in Games

- url: https://eprints.qut.edu.au/44776/
- type: paper
- summary: Sweetser & Wyeth (2005) turn flow theory into an eight-element evaluation model (concentration, challenge, skills, control, goals, feedback, immersion, social).
- reason: The most-cited operational enjoyment rubric in the literature — the closest prior art to this project's deliverable.

## 6. Toward a Theory of Intrinsically Motivating Instruction

- url: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog0504_2
- type: paper
- summary: Malone (1981) derives challenge, fantasy and curiosity as the three intrinsic-motivation categories from empirical study of motivating computer games.
- reason: The earliest empirical "what makes games fun" framework; everything later builds on it.

## 7. A Theory of Fun, 10 Years Later

- url: https://www.raphkoster.com/games/presentations/a-theory-of-fun-10-years-later/
- type: talk
- summary: Koster revisits his thesis that fun is the brain's reward for pattern-learning, with a decade of supporting cognitive-science evidence.
- reason: Anchor for the learning & mastery dimension (the rubric's heaviest weight).

## 8. MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research

- url: https://aaai.org/papers/ws04-04-001-mda-a-formal-approach-to-game-design-and-game-research/
- type: paper
- summary: Hunicke, LeBlanc & Zubek define Mechanics → Dynamics → Aesthetics as a vocabulary bridging design and research.
- reason: Standard decomposition for tracing which mechanics produce which aesthetic the rubric scores.

## 9. Why We Play Games: Four Keys to More Emotion Without Story

- url: https://www.nicolelazzaro.com/the4-keys-to-fun/
- type: post
- summary: Lazzaro's facial-expression research yields Hard, Easy, Serious and People Fun as distinct emotional keys.
- reason: Emotion-based taxonomy; three of four keys apply directly to single-player and structure dimensions 5–7.

## 10. Flow in Games (and Everything Else)

- url: https://www.jenovachen.com/flowingames/p31-chen.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: Chen (CACM 2007) argues for keeping players in a personal Flow Zone via player-driven dynamic difficulty.
- reason: Designer-authored bridge from flow theory to single-player difficulty/pacing criteria.

## 11. The Chemistry of Game Design

- url: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-chemistry-of-game-design
- type: post
- summary: Cook introduces skill atoms (action → simulation → feedback → model) as the learnable unit mechanics chain into.
- reason: Granular vocabulary for scoring how a game teaches and rewards mastery (criterion 1.2).

## 12. The Art of Failure: An Essay on the Pain of Playing Video Games

- url: https://jesperjuul.net/artoffailure/
- type: book
- summary: Juul examines why players seek games despite near-certain failure and the unhappiness it causes.
- reason: Counterweight to fun-as-pleasure; grounds failure-cost and "deserved failure" criteria.

## 13. Juice It or Lose It

- url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg
- type: talk
- summary: Jonasson & Purho live-transform a bare Breakout clone into a juicy one through cascading feedback.
- reason: Canonical demonstration that feedback density multiplies fun independent of mechanics (dimension 4).

## 14. The Gamer Motivation Model (Handy Reference)

- url: https://quanticfoundry.com/2015/12/15/handy-reference/
- type: post
- summary: Yee's 12-motivation model derived from hundreds of thousands of player surveys, linked to design features.
- reason: Large-sample basis for reporting rubric scores per target player-motivation profile.

## 15. Why You Need the Clockwork Game

- url: http://keithburgun.net/why-you-need-the-clockwork-game/
- type: post
- summary: Burgun argues for elegant systems built around a strong core mechanism with prescribed goals, distinct from randomness- or story-driven design.
- reason: Single-player-specific systemic-depth philosophy, including a stance on randomness, missing from emotion/flow sources.
