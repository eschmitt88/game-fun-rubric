## A. Criterion 2.6 (agency)

## 1. Fun and (striving) games: playfulness and agential fluidity
- url: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00948705.2021.1948338
- type: paper
- summary: Ridge extends Nguyen — agency-inhabitation central to fun but needs a distinct "playfulness" stance.
- reason: Direct philosophical engagement with 2.6's sole source.

## 2. JPS special issue on Games: Agency as Art (incl. Nguyen's reply "The Opacity of Play")
- url: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00948705.2021.2007775
- type: paper
- summary: 12 critical papers; coherence-of-agency contested (Vella, Ryall, Behrens) and extended (Ferrero, Pike).
- reason: The corroborate-or-kill source for 2.6.

## 3. "Naked and on Fire": Player Agency Experiences in Narrative-Focused Gameplay
- url: https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3411764.3445540
- type: paper
- summary: CHI 2021 grounded theory — perceived agency is multi-factorial and individual; no single dominant factor.
- reason: Empirical temper for 2.6.

## 4. The Open and the Closed: Games of Emergence and Games of Progression
- url: https://www.jesperjuul.net/text/openandtheclosed.html
- type: paper

- reason: Separates emergent from authored distinctiveness; alternative lens for 2.6/6.2.

## B. Fun vs retention

## 6. How does Juicy Game Feedback Motivate? (CHI 2024)
- url: https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3613904.3642656
- type: paper
- summary: Kao, Ballou, Gerling, Breitsohl & Deterding — pre-registered n=1,699: curiosity strongest predictor of enjoyment AND the only predictor of free-choice playtime; amplified juice REDUCED both via undermined agency.
- reason: The single most rubric-relevant new experiment: hits 4.2 ceiling, 6.3, 2.6 mechanism, and the fun/retention link at once.

## 7. Registered report: no playtime–wellbeing relationship over 3 months
- url: https://tmb.apaopen.org/pub/k8ra4n36/release/1
- type: paper
- summary: Ballou et al. 2024 — 414 players, objective Xbox telemetry, 6 waves: no within-person effect.
- reason: Retention ≠ good experience, objectively tracked.

## 8. Perceived value, not hours played, predicts well-being (Switch)
- url: https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/12/3/241174/86839/Perceived-value-of-video-games-but-not-hours
- type: paper
- summary: Ballou et al. 2025 — 140k logged hours, 703 adults: playtime predicts nothing at any timescale; perceived value does.
- reason: Replication across platform and timescales.

## 9. Video game play is positively correlated with well-being
- url: https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rsos.202049
- type: paper
- summary: Johannes, Vuorre & Przybylski 2021 — EA/Nintendo telemetry+survey; need satisfaction predicts wellbeing independent of playtime.
- reason: The methodological precedent for telemetry+survey linkage.
