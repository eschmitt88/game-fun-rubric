## 1. Level Design Patterns That Invoke Curiosity-Driven Exploration
- url: https://doi.org/10.1145/3474698
- type: paper
- summary: Gómez-Maureira et al. (CHI PLAY 2021, N=254 factorial): curiosity patterns increase exploration, but the effect is crowded out by explicit goals and monetary compensation.
- reason: The largest controlled curiosity-design study; adds the boundary condition Kao 2024 didn't test.

## 2. Exploring Curiosity in Games: A Framework and Questionnaire Study
- url: https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2024.2325171
- type: paper
- summary: Tang & Kirman (IJHCI 2024, N=482): 7 categories / 13 dimensions of game curiosity.
- reason: Structure for dimension 6 if its weight rises.

## 3. From thrill seekers to social creatures (curiosity dimensions)
- url: https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/83317/7/83317.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: Yow et al. (BIT 2024): curiosity dimensions differentially predict game preferences and behaviours.
- reason: Independent multi-dimensional curiosity→behaviour evidence.

## 4. An Empirical Framework for Understanding a Player's Sense of Agency in Games
- url: https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2023.2241286
- type: paper
- summary: Guo & Lo (IJHCI 2023, 18 interviews + N=654): validated 12-item Game Sense of Agency questionnaire (Multisensory Presentation, Feedback Reasoning, Virtual Realism, Control Smoothness).
- reason: Games-specific instrument for the outcome-binding mechanism behind 4.2d and 2.6.

## 5. Sense of Agency and User Experience: Is There a Link?
- url: https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/sense-of-agency-and-user-experience-is-there-a-link/
- type: paper
- summary: Bergström et al. (TOCHI 2022): objective agency measures don't cleanly track subjective control; UX factors spill over.
- reason: Caveat on interpreting Kao 2024's mechanism.

## 6. I did that! Measuring Users' Experience of Agency
- url: https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/i-did-that-measuring-users-experience-of-agency-in-their-own-acti/
- type: paper
- summary: Coyle et al. (CHI 2012): intentional binding imported into HCI.
- reason: Foundational for the comparator-model mechanism.
