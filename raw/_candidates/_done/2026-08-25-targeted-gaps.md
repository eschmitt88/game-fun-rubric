Round 3 closes gaps named in rubric v0.3 "Known gaps" and NOTES.md.

## 1. Juicy Game Design: Understanding the Impact of Visual Embellishments on Player Experience
- url: https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/545000/
- type: paper
- summary: Hicks, Gerling, Dickinson & Vanden Abeele (CHI PLAY 2019): two controlled studies (N=40, N=32); visual embellishments reliably raise visual appeal but affect competence only under specific circumstances.
- reason: The quantitative juice experiment the 4.2↔4.4 gap has been waiting for.

## 2. The Basic Needs in Games Scale (BANGS)
- url: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/212508/1/1-s2.0-S1071581924000739-main.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: Ballou, Denisova, Ryan, Rigby & Deterding (IJHCS 2024, 5 studies, N=1,246): validated 6-subscale measure of need satisfaction AND frustration.
- reason: Instrument for 8.6 / need frustration; supersedes PENS for the playtest protocol.

## 3. The Importance of Challenge for the Enjoyment of Intrinsically Motivated, Goal-Directed Activities
- url: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22067510/
- type: paper
- summary: Abuhamdeh & Csikszentmihalyi (PSPB 2012): in Internet chess, perceived challenge strongly predicts enjoyment and close games beat blowouts; ESM study shows moderation by intrinsic motivation + goal-directedness.
- reason: The missing challenge→enjoyment test with a real measure.

## 4. Effect of Input-output Randomness on Gameplay Satisfaction in Collectable Card Games
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08437
- type: paper
- summary: Zhang et al. (IEEE CoG 2021): controlled 2×2; input randomness (card draw) hurts satisfaction, output randomness (card effects) does not.
- reason: First controlled randomness study in the graph; arbitrates Burgun vs Malone.

## 5. Adaptation in Digital Games: The Effect of Challenge Adjustment on Player Performance and Experience
- url: https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/21352/
- type: paper
- summary: Denisova & Cairns (CHI PLAY 2015): hidden DDA raised immersion vs control, players unaware.
- reason: Contrasts with the DDA null in caroux2023player; immersion, not enjoyment, as outcome.

## 6. The Impact of Tutorials on Games of Varying Complexity
- url: https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: Andersen et al. (CHI 2012, 45,000+ players, 3 games): tutorials raised play time up to 29% only in the most complex game; negligible in simpler ones.
- reason: Large-N experimental evidence for 8.1 — onboarding value is contingent on complexity.

## 7. Revisiting the GameFlow Model with Detailed Heuristics
- url: https://eprints.qut.edu.au/58216/15/JournCT-GameFlow.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: Sweetser, Johnson & Wyeth (J. Creative Technologies 2012): 165 implementable heuristics under the 8 GameFlow elements from grounded analysis of RTS reviews.
- reason: Lineage source for the rubric's anchors; detailed criteria to mine.

## 8. GameFlow 2020: 15 Years of a Model of Player Enjoyment
- url: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/216736/1/OzCHI20_GF2020-1col.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: Sweetser (OzCHI 2020): surveys 205 applications; positions GameFlow as complementary to validated PX scales.
- reason: How the closest prior rubric was actually used and where it failed.

## 9. Flow and Immersion in First-Person Shooters (psychophysiology)
- url: https://scholar.google.com/citations?citation_for_view=i-y5S2AAAAAJ:u5HHmVD_uO8C&hl=en&user=i-y5S2AAAAAJ&view_op=view_citation
- type: paper
- summary: Nacke & Lindley (Future Play 2008): EEG/ECG/EMG/GSR/eye-tracking across boredom/immersion/flow Half-Life 2 mods, cross-validated with questionnaires.
- reason: Objective measures — the behavioural pairing step 4 asks for.

## 10. Don't Juice It or Lose It
- url: https://archive.org/details/GDCEU2014Kelly
- type: talk
- summary: Folmer Kelly (GDC Europe 2014): reflexive juice can undermine immersion by prioritising eye-candy over context.
- reason: The counter-position to jonasson2012juice named in Known gaps.

## 11. Behavioral Game Design
- url: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/behavioral-game-design
- type: post
- summary: Hopson (2001): operant reward schedules and the play-pattern signatures each produces.
- reason: Session shape (3.5) and the fun-vs-compulsion boundary, from the primary source.

## 12. Rethinking Carrots (PENS précis)
- url: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/rethinking-carrots-a-new-method-for-measuring-what-players-find-most-rewarding-and-motivating-about-your-game
- type: post
- summary: Rigby & Ryan (2007): PENS as a stronger predictor of enjoyment and sustained engagement than "fun" ratings; playtest method.
- reason: Stand-in for Glued to Games; the creators' applied framing.

## 13. Games: Agency as Art (Meskin's review of Nguyen)
- url: https://academic.oup.com/jaac/article/79/3/395/6292143
- type: paper
- summary: Nguyen frames games as the art form of agency — designers sculpt temporary agencies players inhabit.
- reason: The toys-vs-tools / autotelic gap; peer-reviewed treatment.

## 14. Large-Scale Cross-Game Player Behavior Analysis on Steam
- url: https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12804/12804-52-16321-1-2-20201228.pdf
- type: paper
- summary: Sifa, Drachen & Bauckhage (AIIDE 2015): 6M players, 3,000+ games; genre/mechanics vs playtime distributions.
- reason: Retention telemetry; same class declined in round 2.

## 15. Game AI Not Fun? Scoping Review and Meta-Analysis
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24749
- type: paper
- summary: Ito (2026): pooled g=.63 enjoyment penalty for computer vs human opponents across 25 effect sizes.
- reason: Recent meta-analysis, but opponent-type is a multiplayer/social variable.
