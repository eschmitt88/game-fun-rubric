# Fun and (striving) games: playfulness and agential fluidity

**This is a combined note.** It is anchored on Ridge's full-text article
(the citekey and primary source) but also covers three companion pieces
from the same *Journal of the Philosophy of Sport* 48(3) (2021) special
issue on nguyen2019games' book *Games: Agency as Art* — Ryall's "Agential
layering, the absurd and the grind in game-playing" (full text obtained)
and Vella's "Beyond agency: games as the aesthetics of being" (abstract-level
only), plus Nguyen's own reply, "The opacity of play: a reply to
commentators" (opening paragraph only) and Yorke's editorial overview of
the issue. **Access asymmetry, stated up front:** Ridge and Ryall were
fully readable (open-access repository copies at the University of
Edinburgh and University of Gloucestershire respectively); Vella and
Nguyen's reply were not — tandfonline.com, philpapers.org and
philarchive.org all 403/Cloudflare-block automated fetch, and the one
confirmed open-access mirror of Nguyen's reply (a Wayback Machine snapshot)
was rate-limited (HTTP 429) throughout this session despite repeated
retries. Raw captures for all four pieces, including the exact fetch
failures, are at `raw/papers/ridge2021fun.pdf`,
`raw/papers/ridge2021fun-supplementary/ryall2021agential.pdf`, and
`raw/papers/ridge2021fun-supplementary/{vella2021beyond,nguyen2021opacity}-abstract.md`.
Everything below attributed to Ridge or Ryall is a direct read of the full
text; everything attributed to Vella or Nguyen's reply is flagged
inline as abstract/secondary-level and should not be treated as a verified
quote.

**One correction to the task brief this note was scoped against**: the
brief named "Vella, Ryall, Behrens" as the critics contesting the
operative variable. There is no "Behrens" in this special issue — the
confirmed 11 commentators (per Yorke's overview, DOI
10.1080/00948705.2021.2007775) are Bartel, Currie, Ferrero, Morgan,
Patridge, Peacocke, Pike, Ridge, Russell, Ryall and Vella. This note
covers the three the brief actually asked about in substance (Ridge,
Vella, Ryall); the other seven are out of scope here.

**Type note (as with nguyen2019games):** all four pieces are analytic
philosophy, not empirical psychology — thought experiments and conceptual
argument, zero playtests or data. Every claim below is capped at **E4 at
best**, same ceiling as nguyen2019games itself. Ridge's paper is fully
peer-reviewed, open access (CC BY-NC-ND), 7 citations in ~5 years
(Semantic Scholar, checked 2026-09-02) — modest but expected for a niche
sub-field reply piece this recent.

## TL;DR

None of the three commentators covered here reject Nguyen's central claim
that a game's *distinctive, coherent, sculpted agency* (goals + permitted
abilities + constraints — the object rubric criterion 2.6 scores) is a
real and important source of value. Ridge and Ryall explicitly affirm it.
Instead they each add a **boundary condition** on when a coherent agency
actually delivers value: Ridge argues coherence is necessary but not
*sufficient* — you also need a **playful stance** (a disposition to hold
the agency lightly and move fluidly in and out of it), or striving play
curdles into the kind of obsessive tunnel vision Sudnow showed for
*Breakout*. Ryall argues the same thing from the opposite direction —
even a maximally distinctive, coherent agency (100+ hours mining rocks in
*Elite Dangerous*) can degrade into "the grind," where the outer,
reflective layer of agency judges the inner layer's continued striving as
pointless, yet the player keeps going anyway (Ryall's "Stockerian
schizophrenic," stuck between agential layers). Vella's title move — value
in games goes "beyond agency" to an aesthetics of *being*/dwelling — is
the one genuine challenge to agency-as-the-operative-variable among the
three, but this note can only report it at abstract/secondary-source
confidence. Nguyen's own reply is inaccessible this session; its
substantive content is an open gap, not evidence either way.

## Claims

### Ridge (full text)

- **Three tensions in Nguyen's theory** (Ridge's framing device for the
  whole paper, p. 1): (1) striving play requires a practically *interested*
  attitude, which is in tension with the *disinterested* attitude usually
  taken to be required for aesthetic appreciation of that same striving;
  (2) "value capture" — gamification exports striving play's motivational
  power into real-world contexts (fitness trackers, habit apps) and, in
  doing so, displaces richer real-world value schemes with simplistic
  game-like ones ("number of steps taken will displace a more plausible
  conception of fitness," p. 3) — motivationally efficacious systems make
  the values they embed "sticky," and long enough heuristic use causes
  "heuristic drift," where the proxy becomes an end in itself; (3) striving
  play (and game play generally) is in tension with **autonomy** — Ridge's
  central cautionary case is McGonigal's retelling of David Sudnow's total
  absorption in *Breakout*: "When I wasn't at the TV, I was practicing the
  sequence in my imagination, walking down the street... just waiting to
  get back to the game" (quoted p. 4) — which Ridge reads as at least
  partly an *impoverishment* of autonomy, not a triumph of striving play.
- **Proposed fix: a fourth "playful stance."** Nguyen's book gives three
  stances (play, spectator, design); Ridge adds **the playful stance**, "in
  which one is poised for playful activity" (p. 4), and argues this is
  conceptually distinct from Nguyen's "play stance" — one can be fully
  *playing a game* (Nguyen's sense) without being the least bit playful
  (Ridge's jaded-footballer example, p. 4), and conversely one can be
  playful ("play full-stop") with no game at all.
- **Definition of play full-stop and of fun**: "An agent is playing
  [full-stop] just in case the agent is engaged in unscripted activity for
  the fun of it" (p. 5). Ridge then offers his own account of fun (flagged
  explicitly as something he "cannot properly defend" here, p. 6): fun is
  a **homeostatic cluster of affective/motivational/perceptual states**
  with characteristic inputs — (a) perceived environmental safety and
  satiety, (b) low anxiety, (c) perception of incongruity, especially
  novelty-in-the-mundane — and characteristic outputs — enjoyment of the
  incongruous element, self-loss/time-loss, amusement, inhibition of
  negative emotion, positive arousal, and a **broadening of perspective
  that primes for creativity**. The unifying function: fun motivates
  *experimental behaviour in safe environments* (p. 6). This draws directly
  on Barbara Fredrickson's broaden-and-build theory of positive emotion
  (1998): positive emotions, unlike fear, don't cue a specific response —
  they broaden the "thought-action repertoire" (p. 7).
- **How playfulness resolves tension 1** (striving vs. aesthetic
  appreciation): a playful chess player can take a genuinely disinterested,
  *unfocused* survey of a position's possible moves — a "Where's Wally?"-style
  scan rather than committed calculation — and this unfocused survey is
  itself a form of **mental agency** (moving a piece "in your head"), so
  playful disinterest doesn't abandon the aesthetics of agency, it enables
  a *purer* aesthetic experience of it (pp. 8-9). Ridge frames this as an
  improvement on, not a refutation of, Nguyen's own "zoom in / zoom out"
  proposed resolution to the same tension (p. 2) — memory-based zooming out
  is too distant and unreliable; real-time playful disinterest is not.
- **How playfulness resolves tension 2** (value capture): playfulness gives
  a *broader perspective* that keeps a game-like heuristic in its place —
  "steps on our Fitbit are just a heuristic, and what we really value is
  our health" (p. 9) — because playfulness's core function is exactly the
  perspective-broadening described in the fun account above.
- **How playfulness resolves tension 3** (autonomy): playfulness
  **enhances agential fluidity** — Nguyen's idea that game-playing gives us
  a "library of agencies" we can draw on in real life requires being
  *motivated to move between* those agencies as context demands, and
  playfulness is what supplies that motivation ("experimenting with new
  approaches... bringing some of the forms of agency developed in playing
  chess to the practice of philosophy," p. 10). Directly opposed to this:
  pure striving play "as such" is *compatible with obsessiveness, tunnel
  vision, and wearing one's (sub-)agency heavily" (p. 11, conclusion) —
  Ridge's explicit summary verdict is that striving play alone is not
  sufficient for the light-footed agency Nguyen himself gestures at in his
  book's closing paragraph (quoted p. 10-11); you need striving play *plus*
  play full-stop (playfulness) for "the best of both worlds" (p. 11).
- **Ridge does not contest the coherent-agency thesis itself.** Explicitly,
  in his opening: "My aim is to help build and enhance the theory... I will
  not try to refute [Nguyen's] theory or pick various nits about the
  details" (p. 1). Chess — a maximally well-defined, coherent, unambiguous
  agential skeleton — is Ridge's chosen central test case throughout,
  precisely *because* its coherence and legibility are not in question,
  which lets him isolate the further, orthogonal question of the player's
  *stance toward* that already-coherent agency.

### Ryall (full text)

- **Opens by affirming Nguyen's thesis outright**: "Nguyen's (2020) core
  thesis is that games are an art form that utilizes the medium of agency
  ... it is the player that takes centre stage... it is the player's
  agency (and their voluntary adoption of an agential role) that is the
  artwork itself... games shape the agential skeleton which the player
  will inhabit for the duration [of] the game; it is the player who puts
  the flesh on the bones" (p. 2, paraphrasing/quoting Nguyen directly).
- **Red Dead Redemption 2 as illustration**: playing as Arthur Morgan
  generated genuine emotional attachment and grief on his (scripted) death,
  to the point that Ryall restarted the game with foreknowledge specifically
  to try to prevent it by staying outside the main storyline — "we are
  striving towards a goal whilst part of us hopes never to reach it" (p. 3).
  This is presented as a paradox *internal to* Nguyen's striving-play
  account, not a challenge to the coherent-agency premise underneath it —
  the paradox only bites *because* RDR2's agency (playing as a specific,
  richly-realized outlaw) is distinctive and coherent enough to sustain
  real attachment.
- **Agential layering, quoted directly from Nguyen (2020, 56)**: "The inner
  agent tries to win. The outer agent has no reason to help the inner
  agent... We have, then, the capacity to submerge ourselves in a temporary
  agency... The outer layer involves those motivations which brought us to
  play the game in the first place... We hide our larger reasons from
  ourselves for a time, submerging ourselves in the inner layer." Ryall
  also quotes Nguyen's direct response to Stocker's (1976) "motivational
  schizophrenia" charge against nested/layered agency: "Striving players
  are, in a crucial sense, Stockerian schizophrenics. We take up and
  discard temporary agencies whose commitments and ends do not match up
  with those of our full selves" (Nguyen 2020, 60, quoted p. 4).
- **The grind, Ryall's central original contribution**: even inside a
  well-sculpted, distinctive, coherent agency, sustained play can decay
  into repetitive, mundane, purely instrumental activity — "grinding" —
  which she distinguishes sharply from *play*, characterizing it as closer
  to *work*, exactly the kind of activity Suits' Utopia thought experiment
  was designed to eliminate (p. 6). Her own example: "100+ hours in the
  mind-numbing process of mining rocks in the game Elite Dangerous so that
  I can trade them to buy a bigger ship that is capable of mining more
  rocks" (p. 7) — a *maximally* distinctive, non-generic, game-specific
  agency (mining-and-trading in a space-sim), yet one whose sustained
  practice she calls absurd once the outer, reflective layer of agency
  looks back on it: "my outer agency asks, 'what have I been doing with my
  time?'... it becomes problematic and absurd when we find ourselves stuck
  between layers: when our outer layer wants to pull us away but our inner
  layer wants to continue. It is perhaps the exemplar of the Stockerian
  schizophrenic" (p. 7). Cites Nagel's "The Absurd" (1971) and Suits'
  "Alexandrian condition of man" ("When there are no more worlds to
  conquer we are filled not with satisfaction but despair," Suits 2014,
  189, quoted p. 7).
- **Conclusion explicitly re-affirms the coherent-agency thesis** while
  flagging the grind as its shadow: "Nguyen is right in noting the
  importance and uniqueness of agency in games. The best games are those
  which allow us to stretch our abilities but also our imagination...
  However, games may also force us to confront the absurdity of our lives
  in a way that other activities do not, since we are aware that the goal
  towards which we are striving is a temporary one that we may choose to
  ignore. It is 'grinding' in games that perhaps demonstrates the extent of
  this absurdity the most" (p. 8, conclusion). She offers RDR2 as the
  positive counter-case: "The game itself is rich enough, and the agential
  moves numerous enough, that the likelihood of repetitive or unengaging
  tasks is diminished" (p. 7) — i.e. richness/coherence of the sculpted
  agency is what *protects against* the grind, not what causes it.

### Vella (abstract/secondary-level only — flagged, not a verified quote)

- Title move: "**Beyond** agency: games as the aesthetics of being" —
  argues (per the recoverable abstract fragment, corroborated by a
  WebSearch-tool synthesis of indexed secondary commentary, itself
  paraphrase-level) that games' aesthetic value is not exhausted by the
  agency-medium framing and proposes an aesthetics of **being** — modes of
  existence/dwelling the game world affords.
- Per the same secondary synthesis (**unverified against primary text**):
  Vella's paper "offered additional texture to Nguyen's phenomenological
  landscape, emphasizing the many meaningful non-striving actions that
  players can take up in certain types of games, such as trying on new
  digital skins, languid searching for the best in-game vistas, or acting
  out side quests of their own design" — i.e. locates value in dwelling
  and open-ended exploration that is not goal-directed striving at all,
  and so is not obviously scoreable by an agency-coherence criterion.
  See `raw/papers/ridge2021fun-supplementary/vella2021beyond-abstract.md`
  for the full account of what could and could not be recovered.

## Methods

Pure philosophical argument throughout (thought experiments, conceptual
analysis, close engagement with Nguyen 2020's book) — same method as
nguyen2019games. Ridge draws additionally on empirical positive-psychology
theory (Fredrickson 1998) for his account of fun's function, but does not
run or cite any new empirical study himself. Ryall draws on a single
illustrative video-game case each for her positive (RDR2) and negative
(Elite Dangerous grind) examples, plus a real-world sport case (an
interminable, perfectly-matched tennis rally that becomes boring despite
being "the ideal game" in theory, p. 3) — illustrative, not data.

## Results

Not applicable in the empirical sense (see nguyen2019games's note for the
same caveat). The "results" here are argumentative: Ridge shows Nguyen's
three-stance taxonomy needs a fourth (playful) stance to resolve three
internal tensions in the book; Ryall shows agential layering has a
predictable failure mode (the grind) even when the underlying agency is
maximally distinctive and coherent; Vella (unverified) proposes an
aesthetic category orthogonal to agency altogether.

## Critique / open questions

- **Zero empirical grounding, same as nguyen2019games** — none of this
  adjudicates whether difficulty, juice, or DDA move enjoyment; capped at
  E4 throughout.
- **Ridge's own account of fun is explicitly underdefended** ("which I
  cannot properly defend here," p. 6) — it's offered as a working
  hypothesis, not a settled theory, and should be read as *suggestive*
  framing for the rubric's "fun" construct, not as a citable definition on
  a par with, e.g., PXI/PENS's validated instruments.
- **Access asymmetry is a real limitation of this note.** Two of the four
  pieces (Vella, Nguyen's reply) are represented at abstract/secondary
  confidence only. If Vella's "beyond agency" argument turns out to be a
  strong, well-argued rejection (rather than an extension) of
  agency-as-operative-variable, that would matter more for rubric
  dimension 2's framing than anything captured here — this is flagged as a
  concrete follow-up, not resolved.
- **Ryall's grind critique overlaps, but is not identical to**, the
  rubric's existing 3.5 × dimension-2 compulsion cross-read
  (hopson2001behavioral's valence-neutral operant-conditioning account,
  ADR 0005). Hopson's mechanism is behaviourist (variable-ratio reward
  schedules are valence-neutral, full stop); Ryall's is phenomenological
  (the *player's own reflective outer agency* judges the inner agency's
  continued activity as absurd, yet the activity continues) — two
  independent routes to the same practical warning (a distinctive,
  coherent agency does not by itself guarantee the player is having a
  *good* time sustaining it), which is worth stating explicitly in the
  rubric rather than leaving as an implicit overlap.
- **Ridge's playfulness is a *player disposition/stance*, not obviously a
  scoreable *design* property** — this matters for whether it belongs in
  the rubric at all. A design can *support* the playful stance (low
  stakes, reversibility, no punishing time pressure on experimentation —
  arguably already covered by existing criteria like 3.2 failure-cost
  calibration and 6.4 experimentation-is-rewarded) without guaranteeing any
  given player takes it up. Best read as reinforcing existing criteria
  rather than motivating a new one.
- **Internal tension worth flagging, echoing nguyen2019games's note**:
  Ryall's "stupid games are for striving players" point isn't at issue
  here, but her grind example (Elite Dangerous) and Ridge's obsession
  example (Sudnow/Breakout) are both drawn from *serious*, high-investment
  play — reinforcing the earlier note's observation that striving-play
  pathologies are not confined to "casual" or "stupid" games.

## Rubric implications

- **2.6 (Distinctive, coherent agency)** — the ADR 0005 directive was
  "corroborate or drop by v0.6." This note, combined with
  `concepts/games-as-art-of-agency.md`'s existing corroboration from
  carstensdottir2021naked (a 28-participant interview study — the first
  *empirical*, not just philosophical, support for 2.6's premise), moves
  2.6 from single-philosophical-source (nguyen2019games alone) to
  **corroborated by four independent sources within the same peer-reviewed
  forum plus one independent empirical study**. Tier stays E4 (still zero
  new data from *this* note specifically), but single-source risk is
  resolved well ahead of the v0.6 deadline. **No commentator covered here
  argues against scoring distinctiveness/coherence of agency** —
  Ridge and Ryall both explicitly affirm it as a real source of value; only
  Vella (lowest-confidence source in this note) proposes going beyond it,
  and only for non-striving/exploratory value that dimension 2 was never
  scoped to capture in the first place (it scores *agency and meaningful
  choice*, not dwelling/aesthetic-of-being).
- **Recommend appending, not rewording, 2.6's evidence-status line**: cite
  ridge2021fun and (with the explicit abstract-only/secondary caveat)
  Ryall and Vella's 2021 pieces alongside nguyen2019games and
  carstensdottir2021naked. The criterion's core wording ("goals + permitted
  abilities + constraints form a recognizable mode of being... not a
  generic decision tree") is unchallenged by anything read this session.
- **Recommend a one-line cross-reference from 2.6 to 3.5's compulsion
  cross-read**, worded roughly: *"A distinctive, coherent agency does not
  by itself protect against 3.5's compulsion signature — see
  ryall2021agential's 'grind' (a maximally coherent agency, e.g. sustained
  mining/trading loops, can still curdle into absurd, joyless repetition)
  and ridge2021fun's playful stance (a proposed player-side antidote:
  designs that support low-stakes, reversible experimentation — already
  partially covered by 3.2 and 6.4 — help keep striving play light-footed
  rather than obsessive)."* This does not require a new criterion row; it
  strengthens the existing cross-read with two new, independent
  philosophical sources beyond hopson2001behavioral's behaviourist account.
- **New, distinct known-gap (not a 2.6 fix)**: flag Vella's "beyond agency"
  challenge as an open question for whether the rubric needs *any*
  mechanism for non-agency, dwelling-type value (open-world wandering,
  vista-seeking, cosmetic self-authorship not tied to striving) — this is
  plausibly out of scope for dimension 2 specifically (which is titled
  *agency & meaningful choice*) and might belong instead as a
  documented limitation alongside the existing "measures fun, not meaning"
  framing paragraph, or as fresh territory for dimension 6 (novelty,
  curiosity, discovery) or 7 (emotion, fantasy, narrative). **Do not
  resolve this without the full Vella text** — abstract-level confidence
  is too low to justify a rubric change on its own.
- **No change recommended to weights or to any other dimension.** Nothing
  in any of the four sources is quantitative.

## VERDICT for rubric 2.6

**Refined, not simply corroborated and not dropped.** Every commentator
this note could read in full — Ridge and Ryall — explicitly affirms
Nguyen's core claim that a game's distinctive, coherent, sculpted agency is
a genuine and important source of value; neither offers grounds to drop
2.6 or to doubt that "coherence/distinctiveness of the agential skeleton"
is a real, scoreable design property. What they add is a **boundary
condition 2.6 does not currently state**: coherence is necessary but not
sufficient for that value to actually land as *good* experience for the
player — it can curdle into obsessive tunnel vision (Ridge, via Sudnow's
*Breakout*) or absurd, joyless grind (Ryall, via 100+ hours of Elite
Dangerous mining) even when the underlying agency is maximally distinctive
and non-generic. Both failure modes are best read as sharpening the
rubric's *existing* 3.5 × dimension-2 compulsion cross-read (ADR 0005),
not as motivating a new criterion row. Vella's "beyond agency" position is
the one source here that could genuinely contest 2.6's framing rather than
extend it, but this note can only report it at abstract/secondary
confidence — it is flagged as an open question, not adjudicated. Nguyen's
own reply remains unread; treat the "corroborate or drop by v0.6" question
as **provisionally resolved toward corroboration** (four independent
philosophical sources plus carstensdottir2021naked's empirical leg) but
not fully closed until Vella's full text and Nguyen's reply are recovered.
**Proposed wording**: keep 2.6's criterion text unchanged; update its
evidence-status annotation to *"E4, corroborated within-forum by
ridge2021fun and ryall2021agential (full text) and, at lower confidence,
Vella (2021, abstract-level); one independent empirical corroboration via
carstensdottir2021naked (N=28 interview study)"*; and add the one-line
cross-reference to 3.5 proposed above under "Rubric implications."
