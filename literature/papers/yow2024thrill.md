# From thrill seekers to social creatures: dimensions of curiosity differentially predict video game preferences and behaviours

## TL;DR

Cross-sectional correlational study (N=398 self-identified video gamers,
Malaysia + Philippines, mean age 37.30) that decomposes curiosity into the
**Five-Dimensional Curiosity Scale Revised (5DCR; Kashdan et al. 2020)** —
joyous exploration, deprivation sensitivity, stress tolerance, thrill
seeking, overt social curiosity, covert social curiosity — and regresses
each dimension against (a) self-reported hours spent gaming and (b)
enjoyment ratings of the five King/Delfabbro/Griffiths (2010) video-game
**structural-characteristic** categories (social, manipulation-and-control,
narrative-and-identity, reward-and-punishment, presentation), via the
**Video Game Structural Characteristics Survey (VGSC)**. Headline result:
of six curiosity dimensions, only **thrill seeking** significantly
predicted overall playtime, and the model explained almost none of the
variance (R²=.02). **Joyous exploration** — the 5DCR subscale closest to
the epistemic/information-gap curiosity construct this rubric's dimension 6
is built on — predicted enjoyment of reward-and-punishment features but was
*not* a significant predictor of playtime (p=.491). Overt (not covert)
social curiosity predicted enjoyment of social features.

## Claims

- All six 5DCR dimensions were hypothesised to predict playtime; only one
  did (thrill seeking), and weakly — "our hypothesis was only weakly
  supported" (§8.2.1, authors' own words).
- Joyous exploration was hypothesised to predict enjoyment of
  reward-and-punishment features; this hypothesis **was** supported.
- Both overt and covert social curiosity were hypothesised to predict
  enjoyment of social features; only overt was significant — "partially
  supported."
- Exploratory (non-pre-registered) findings: thrill seeking predicted
  enjoyment of 4 of 5 structural-characteristic categories (all but
  reward-and-punishment); joyous exploration additionally predicted
  manipulation-and-control and presentation features; deprivation
  sensitivity predicted *none* of the five categories.
- 5DCR's six-factor structure and ≥.80 subscale alphas were previously
  confirmed in German and UK samples (Grüning & Lechner 2023); this
  study's own alphas ranged .82–.90.

## Methods

- Design: online cross-sectional survey (Qualtrics), correlational, not
  experimental — no manipulation, no behavioural/objective playtime
  measure (self-reported retrospective recall only, "average hours per
  day" across weekday/weekend, capped at 24h/day at cleaning).
- Sample: recruited via panel provider, USD$3 incentive; N=398 after
  cleaning (18 nonsensical device responses dropped separately), ≥18y,
  proficient in English, ≥1h/week play. 50.30% male/49.70% female,
  M age=37.30 (SD=12.05), 50.80% Philippines / 49.20% Malaysia. Platform
  skew: 73.06% of single-platform gamers were mobile-only (~75% of the
  full sample plays on mobile at all).
- Instruments: 5DCR (24 items, 4/subscale, 7-pt Likert, reverse-scored
  stress tolerance); VGSC (37 items across 5 features, 5-pt enjoyment
  Likert, "not enjoyable at all" to "very enjoyable").
- Analysis: (1) stepwise forward multiple linear regression, hours-played
  ~ 6 curiosity dimensions; (2) multivariate multiple regression, 5
  structural-characteristic enjoyment scores ~ 6 curiosity dimensions
  (30 coefficients in Table 4, no stated correction for multiple
  comparisons beyond the omnibus F test).

## Results

- **Playtime model**: R²=.02, F(1,396)=8.38, p=.004. Thrill seeking:
  B=2.11, SE=0.73, β=.14, t(396)=2.89, p=.004. All other five dimensions
  non-significant, including joyous exploration (β=−.04, p=.491),
  deprivation sensitivity (β=−.01, p=.833), stress tolerance (β=−.08,
  p=.103), overt social curiosity (β=.02, p=.675), covert social
  curiosity (β=.07, p=.189).
- **Structural-characteristics model**: overall F(30,1955)=6.38, p<.001.
  Confirmatory results: joyous exploration → reward-and-punishment
  (B=0.08, SE=0.04, p=.034); overt social curiosity → social features
  (B=0.10, SE=0.04, p=.019); covert social curiosity → social features,
  ns (B=0.02, SE=0.03, p=.439).
- Exploratory significant coefficients (Table 4, all p<.05): joyous
  exploration → social (B=.13, p=.003), manipulation-and-control (B=.09,
  p=.033), presentation (B=.09, p=.037); stress tolerance → social
  (B=.11, p<.001), reward-and-punishment (B=.06, p=.010); thrill seeking →
  social (B=.10, p=.009), manipulation-and-control (B=.07, p=.027),
  narrative-and-identity (B=.11, p=.006), presentation (B=.09, p=.015);
  covert social curiosity → narrative-and-identity (B=.09, p=.002).
  Deprivation sensitivity: zero significant associations across all five
  categories.

## Critique / open questions

- **Not a replication of kao2024how's curiosity→playtime finding, and in
  one respect a mild disconfirmation.** kao2024how (pre-registered SEM,
  N=1,699, experimentally manipulated info-gap curiosity within one game)
  found curiosity the *strongest* predictor of enjoyment and the *only*
  significant predictor of voluntary free-choice playtime (β≈.75–.87).
  Here, the 5DCR subscale closest to that same epistemic/info-gap
  construct — **joyous exploration** — was **not** a significant predictor
  of (retrospective, self-reported) overall playtime (p=.491); the
  playtime effect that *did* appear came from **thrill seeking**, a
  distinct dispositional dimension (arousal/risk-seeking) that does not
  map onto dimension 6's information-gap mechanism at all — it sits closer
  to the rubric's fiero/hard-fun (5.5) or striving-play framing than to
  curiosity-as-discovery. The two studies also differ on every axis that
  matters for comparability: trait/dispositional curiosity (5DCR) vs.
  state/manipulated in-game curiosity (kao2024how); retrospective
  cross-game recall vs. objective free-choice playtime after a controlled
  session; R²=.02 (near-null explanatory power) vs. a fitted SEM with
  large standardized paths. Net read: **adjacent, not a replication.** It
  neither confirms nor cleanly refutes kao2024how — it is evidence that
  *trait* curiosity (joyous exploration) is a weak-to-absent predictor of
  *aggregate* playtime, which is a different claim from kao2024how's about
  *state* curiosity and *free-choice* playtime within one play session.
  ADR 0006's "if replicated in a second game, raise dimension 6" trigger
  should **not** fire on this source; if anything it is a caution against
  over-generalizing kao2024how's playtime result to trait-level, cross-game
  curiosity.
- **Effect sizes throughout are small.** The playtime model explains 2% of
  variance; individual structural-characteristic coefficients (B≈.02–.13
  on a 5-point scale) are modest even where significant.
- **30 coefficients tested in Table 4 with no stated multiple-comparison
  correction** beyond the omnibus multivariate F — some of the p≈.03–.04
  exploratory hits (e.g. joyous exploration → manipulation-and-control,
  p=.033) should be read cautiously.
- **Self-report only, no behavioural measure** — directly the pattern this
  project's own doctrine (`docs/rubric.md` "Fun vs retention" section,
  S3 caution) flags as unreliable; retrospective playtime recall is a
  weaker measure than kao2024how's logged free-choice minutes.
- **Sample is narrow and mobile-skewed**: Malaysia + Philippines only,
  ~75% mobile gamers; authors explicitly flag limited generalisability,
  especially for presentation-feature findings (graphics/audio fidelity
  differs by platform).
- **Deprivation sensitivity's null result across every DV** is itself a
  useful negative: the 5DCR's "closing an anxiety-inducing information
  gap" facet tracked nothing here, whereas joyous exploration ("interest
  in and openness to novel experience") is the facet that did the
  (limited) work — some support for treating joyous exploration, not
  deprivation sensitivity, as the closer analogue to this rubric's
  curiosity construct.
- **Social curiosity findings are largely out of this rubric's scope**
  (single-player, genre-agnostic per project CLAUDE.md); noted for
  completeness but not pursued into a new concept.

## Trust signals

- **Credibility: 3** — peer-reviewed (Behaviour & Information Technology),
  open access (CC BY-NC-ND), ethics-approved (JCU H8712), validated
  instruments with sample-specific alphas reported (.82–.90 for 5DCR,
  .75–.91 for VGSC), pre-registration-adjacent hypothesis/exploratory
  split honestly labelled. Downgraded from a higher score by: purely
  correlational design with a near-null playtime model (R²=.02), stepwise
  regression (methodologically contested for inflating false positives),
  no multiple-comparison correction across 30 exploratory coefficients,
  self-report-only measures (no behavioural corroboration), and a narrow,
  mobile-skewed, two-country convenience sample with an authors'-own
  generalisability caveat.

## Follow-up

- If a second experimentally-controlled, in-game (state) curiosity study
  replicates kao2024how's playtime finding, that — not this trait-level
  survey — is what should fire ADR 0006's dimension-6 reweighting trigger.
- Grüning & Lechner (2023) German/UK 5DCR validation could be worth a
  citekey if this project ever wants the instrument's psychometric
  provenance independent of this application paper.
