# The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self-Determination Theory Approach

## TL;DR

Four studies apply Self-Determination Theory (SDT) to video game play using a
new instrument, the Player Experience of Need Satisfaction (PENS). Across a
single-game lab study, a two-game comparison, a four-game multilevel study,
and a 730-person MMO survey, in-game **autonomy** and **competence** (and, in
multiplayer contexts, **relatedness**) consistently and independently predict
enjoyment and preference for future play; **presence/immersion** is itself
predicted by autonomy and competence rather than being an independent driver;
and **intuitive controls** matter only *instrumentally* — their effect on
enjoyment/motivation is fully mediated by the competence and autonomy they
enable, not a direct effect of "ease of use" itself.

## Claims

- SDT's Cognitive Evaluation Theory (CET) predicts that game features
  enhancing perceived **autonomy** (volition, choice over goals/strategies)
  and **competence** (optimal challenge, positive feedback, mastery) will
  increase intrinsic motivation for play (p.4, "Autonomy and competence").
- **Presence** (Lombard & Ditton's "illusion of non-mediation") is
  hypothesized and shown to be *fostered by* perceived autonomy and
  competence, not an independent third pillar of motivation for single-player
  games (p.4-5, "Presence"; confirmed Study 1 Table 2, Study 3 Table 7).
- **Intuitive controls (IC)** — "the price of admission" (quoting Rigby,
  2004) — facilitate competence and autonomy but do not themselves directly
  drive enjoyment/motivation once competence and autonomy are accounted for;
  full mediation shown via Baron & Kenny (1986) regressions in Studies 1 and 2
  (p.6, Table 3; p.9, Table 5).
- IC only predicted outcomes in the *preferred* game (Zelda) in Study 2, not
  in the less-preferred game (A Bug's Life) — good controls are necessary but
  not sufficient; motivation requires that the game "afford opportunities for
  need satisfaction in order for controls to be associated with positive
  effects" (p.10, Study 2 discussion).
- Study 4 (MMO survey, n=730): SDT's three needs — autonomy, competence, and
  **relatedness** — each independently predict enjoyment, future play
  intentions, and (relatedness + competence) hours/week played, even after
  controlling for Yee's (2005, in press) three-factor achievement/social/
  immersion motive taxonomy (p.14-15, Table 9). Relatedness strongly converges
  with Yee's "social" motive (r=.66) and presence with Yee's "immersion"
  motive (r=.57), supporting construct validity of both frameworks.
- Yee's **achievement** motive is *negatively* associated with post-play mood
  (β=-.21, p<.01) despite positively predicting hours/week (β=.19, p<.01) —
  the authors read this as evidence that competitive/power motives can carry
  a psychological cost even while sustaining engagement (p.15, general
  discussion).
- Short-term well-being changes (pre- to post-play) are mixed and
  need-satisfaction-dependent, not uniformly positive or negative: vitality
  dropped on average across studies, but players who experienced competence
  and/or autonomy showed *more positive* pre-post change in mood,
  self-esteem, and vitality than those who did not (Study 1 pp.5-6; Study 3
  Table 7).

## Methods

Four studies, all applying the same PENS instrument (subscales: in-game
autonomy, in-game competence, in-game relatedness [Study 4 only], intuitive
controls, presence) plus outcome measures (game enjoyment [IMI-derived],
preference for future play, a behavioral continued-play choice [Studies 1-2],
and well-being change scores: subjective vitality, state self-esteem, mood).

- **Study 1** (n=89 undergrads, 23M/66F): single game (*Super Mario 64*), one
  20-min session. Simultaneous multiple regression of outcomes onto autonomy
  + competence; Baron & Kenny mediation of IC → outcomes via autonomy/
  competence.
- **Study 2** (n=50 undergrads, 14M/36F): within-subjects, two 3D-adventure
  games pre-selected by GameRankings.com popularity to differ in engagement
  (*Zelda: Ocarina of Time*, 97.8% rating, vs. *A Bug's Life*, 56.6%),
  counterbalanced order, 2-7 days apart, 40-min sessions each. Repeated-
  measures ANOVA on differences between games; difference-score regressions;
  per-game mediation regressions.
- **Study 3** (n=58 undergrads, 12M/46F): within-subjects, four games from
  different genres pre-selected for high in-genre popularity (*Super Mario
  64*, *Super Smash Brothers*, *Star Fox 64*, *San Francisco Rush*), 2-7 days
  apart, 40-min sessions. Hierarchical Linear Modeling (HLM; Bryk &
  Raudenbush, 1992) to separate within-person (game-to-game, L1) from
  between-person (trait-like, L2) effects.
- **Study 4** (n=730, 679M/51F, mean age 22.1): cross-sectional online survey
  of an MMO-gaming community (self-selected, incentivized by raffle), no
  laboratory manipulation. Adds an in-game relatedness PENS subscale and
  Yee's Motivation Components Measure (achievement/social/immersion) for
  comparison. Multiple regression of outcomes (future play, hours/week,
  enjoyment, post-play mood) onto the six motivation predictors
  simultaneously, controlling for gender.

All PENS subscales used 7-point Likert items; internal consistencies (alpha)
ranged .63-.95 across studies (competence and autonomy generally .66-.79;
IC as low as .63 in Study 4's 3-item short form).

## Results

Reporting standardized betas (β) or, where noted, standardized B from
difference-score regressions. `**` = p<.01, `*` = p<.05 unless stated.

**Study 1** (Table 2/3, simultaneous regression of outcomes onto autonomy &
competence):
- Enjoyment: autonomy β=.49**, competence β=.34**
- Preference for future play: autonomy β=.38**, competence β=.39**
- Free-choice continued play (behavioral): autonomy β=-.04 (ns), competence
  β=.41*
- Presence: autonomy β=.19 (ns), competence β=.39**
- IC → autonomy β=.37**, IC → competence β=.40**
- Well-being change: self-esteem ~ competence β=.52**; mood ~ autonomy
  β=.26*, ~competence β=.25*; vitality ns for both.
- Mediation: IC's raw effect on preference-for-continued-play (step 1
  β=.57**) and enjoyment (step 1 β=.64**) drops to non-significance (β=.13,
  .18) once autonomy and competence are entered (step 3), which remain
  significant (β=.33**/.33** and .38**/.31** respectively) — full mediation.

**Study 2** (Zelda vs. A Bug's Life, n=50, within-subjects):
- Zelda > A Bug's Life on enjoyment (M=4.56 vs 3.60, F(1,48)=25.11, p<.001),
  preference for future play (3.21 vs 2.24, F(1,48)=24.75, p<.001), and
  presence (2.82 vs 2.40, F(1,48)=6.96, p<.05). IC did *not* differ between
  games (F(1,48)=0.32, ns).
- Games differed in autonomy (F(1,48)=8.89, p<.01) and competence
  (F(1,48)=8.52, p<.01).
- Difference-score regressions (game preference explained by need
  satisfaction): future play ~ autonomy B=.41**, competence B=.54**;
  enjoyment ~ autonomy B=.35**, competence B=.54**; presence ~ autonomy
  B=.45**, competence B=.40**.
- Per-game (Table 4): in Zelda, IC strongly predicted competence (β=.52**)
  but not autonomy (β=.20 ns); in A Bug's Life similarly (IC→competence
  β=.43**, IC→autonomy β=-.02 ns).
- Mediation (Zelda only — presence not significant enough in A Bug's Life to
  run): IC's raw effect on preference for future play (step1 β=.50**) drops
  to β=.12 (ns) once autonomy (β=.05 ns) and competence (β=.57**) are
  entered; same pattern for enjoyment (step1 β=.42** → step2 IC β=-.01 ns,
  competence β=.46**).

**Study 3** (4 games, n=58, HLM within/between-person):
- Within-person (L1, i.e. game-to-game variation): autonomy and competence
  both predicted enjoyment (β=.76**/.76**), preference for future play
  (β=.96**/.65**), presence (β=.35**/.21*), and value-for-play
  (β=.38**/.50**). Within-person well-being change was driven mainly by
  **competence**: Δvitality β=.37**, Δself-esteem β=.07*, Δmood β=.20**
  (autonomy non-significant on all three).
- Between-person (L2, i.e. trait differences across the sample): overall
  enjoyment predicted by both autonomy (β=.55**) and competence (β=.35**);
  overall preference for future play (β=.66**) and value-for-play increase
  (β=.85**) predicted only by autonomy; overall presence predicted only by
  competence (β=.46*). Δself-esteem (β=.60**) and Δmood (β=.43*) at trait
  level were predicted by autonomy; Δvitality (β=.57**) by competence.

**Study 4** (MMO survey, n=730, simultaneous regression, Table 9):
- Enjoyment (F=97.37**, R²=.45): autonomy β=.49**, competence β=.24**,
  relatedness β=.12**; Yee achievement/social/immersion all non-significant.
- Preference for future play (F=10.91**, R²=.10): autonomy β=.15**,
  competence β=.14**, relatedness β=.12**; Yee variables non-significant.
- Hours/week played (F=15.06**, R²=.11): relatedness β=.18**, Yee
  achievement β=.19**, competence β=.09*; autonomy non-significant.
- Post-play mood (F=32.06**, R²=.21): autonomy β=.36**, competence β=.12**;
  Yee achievement β=-.21** (negative); relatedness non-significant.
- Zero-order correlations (Table 8): autonomy-competence r=.45**,
  competence-relatedness r=.45**, autonomy-relatedness r=.25**;
  relatedness-Yee social r=.66**; presence-Yee immersion r=.57**;
  autonomy-Yee achievement r=.03 (ns) — strongest cross-model convergence is
  relatedness/social and presence/immersion.
- Gender: men scored higher on achievement (F(1,729)=16.53, p<.01) and
  immersion (F(1,729)=16.25, p<.01), lower on social (F(1,729)=7.19, p<.01).

## Critique / open questions

- **Lab studies use forced/assigned exposure**, not voluntary self-selected
  play — the authors themselves flag this (general discussion, p.16) as
  a limitation; Study 4's survey is voluntary/self-selected but purely
  correlational (no causal claims possible), and sampled almost entirely men
  (679/730) from one MMO-community forum, limiting generalization.
- **Dated stimulus games** (Nintendo 64 titles from 1996-1999: Mario 64,
  Zelda: OoT, Smash Bros, Star Fox 64, SF Rush, plus PC's A Bug's Life) —
  these are simple by modern standards; whether the same competence/autonomy
  effect sizes hold for contemporary genres (open-world, live-service,
  narrative-heavy) is untested here and is exactly the gap this project's
  rubric needs to watch for.
- **Session lengths are short** (20-40 minutes) — the paper only measures
  short-term well-being change, explicitly disclaiming any claim about
  long-term effects, addiction, or aggression.
- **No experimental manipulation of difficulty/challenge** — despite framing
  presence as flow-adjacent (citing Csikszentmihalyi via Lombard & Ditton),
  the studies never vary challenge level or measure challenge-skill balance
  directly; the "flow" connection is asserted via presence, not tested.
- **IC alpha is low in places** (.63-.66 in several administrations,
  3-item short forms) — the "intuitive controls" measurement itself is the
  weakest-validated PENS subscale used.
- **Competence is consistently the strongest or co-strongest predictor**
  across all four studies, arguably more consistently than autonomy
  (especially for well-being change and for MMO hours/week) — the paper's
  narrative emphasizes "autonomy AND competence" symmetrically, but the
  data lean competence-forward for single-player games specifically, autonomy-
  forward for MMO enjoyment/mood.
- No code/data/materials release (expected for a 2006 psych paper); PENS
  item wording is quoted in-text but the full instrument isn't appended.

## Trust signals

- **Credibility: 5** — Ryan is a co-founder of Self-Determination Theory
  itself; peer-reviewed original-research article in *Motivation and
  Emotion* (Springer); 3,261 citations (Semantic Scholar, DOI
  10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8, checked 2026-08-25) — this is the founding
  empirical paper for the PENS instrument used throughout subsequent
  games-and-SDT literature (e.g. Tyack & Mekler 2020, Przybylski et al.
  2010). Four independent studies with different designs (single-game lab,
  within-subject comparison, multilevel, large survey) converging on the
  same core result is unusually strong for a single paper.

## Rubric implications

- **Dimension 2 — Agency & meaningful choice (15%)**: directly supported.
  This is the canonical PENS/autonomy citation the rubric already names as
  a primary source. Autonomy independently predicts enjoyment/future-play in
  all 4 studies (e.g. Study 3 L1 β=.76**/.96**). Confirms the dimension
  belongs in the rubric; does not by itself justify the specific 15% weight.
- **Dimension 1 — Learning & mastery (20%)**: supported indirectly.
  Competence is the single most consistent predictor across studies —
  including being the *sole* driver of within-person well-being change
  (Study 3: Δvitality/Δself-esteem/Δmood all ~competence, not autonomy) and
  of MMO hours/week (Study 4) — which is directionally consistent with
  weighting mastery/competence (Dim 1, 20%) above agency/autonomy (Dim 2,
  15%), though the paper doesn't test "pattern learning" (Koster's specific
  claim) directly — it operationalizes competence as felt effectance/
  challenge, not skill progression per se.
- **Dimension 8 — Clarity & friction (5%), esp. 8.3 "Rules are learnable"**:
  strong, specific, and currently uncited support. IC (controls
  intuitiveness) has **no direct effect on enjoyment/motivation** — its
  effect is fully mediated by the competence/autonomy it enables (Study 1
  Table 3, Study 2 Table 5), and IC only mattered in the *preferred* game in
  Study 2. This is exactly the rubric's existing framing ("mostly a
  subtractor: bad usability removes fun without adding it") — propose citing
  this paper as the empirical anchor for that sentence, and consider a
  design note under 8.3: controls should be evaluated as an *enabler* of
  competence/autonomy scores, not scored as independently additive to fun.
- **Dimension 3 — Challenge-skill balance & flow (15%)**: weak/indirect
  support only. Presence (used as a flow proxy) is predicted by competence
  and autonomy but the studies never manipulate or measure challenge-skill
  matching directly — do not cite this paper as direct evidence for 3.1
  (difficulty curve); it's evidence for the needs *underlying* flow, not
  flow's structural precondition.
- **Social/relatedness (currently out of scope per rubric's "Known gaps")**:
  Study 4 shows relatedness is an *independent* predictor of enjoyment
  (β=.12**), future play (β=.12**), and especially hours/week (β=.18**,
  MMO context) even controlling for autonomy, competence, and Yee's
  motives. Supports the rubric's own note that a social dimension exists but
  should stay unweighted for the single-player scope — no change to v0.1,
  but worth flagging for the eventual multiplayer/genre-reweighting pass.
- **No new criterion proposed.** The paper validates autonomy and competence
  as empirically distinct (r=.45 in Study 4 — correlated but not redundant),
  which supports keeping Dimensions 1 and 2 as separate rubric rows rather
  than merging them.

## Follow-up

- **Relevance: 5** — this is the primary empirical anchor named in this
  project's `CLAUDE.md` for the SDT/PENS framework underpinning Dimension 2
  (and materially informing Dimension 1's competence weighting and
  Dimension 8's "controls as subtractor" framing); it directly seeds/anchors
  `need-satisfaction-sdt-pens` in the shared concept vocabulary.
- Worth a follow-up ingest of Przybylski, Rigby & Ryan (2010, "A
  Motivational Model of Video Game Engagement") and Tyack & Mekler (2020),

  samples — would let the rubric cite effect sizes from contemporary titles
  rather than 1996-1999 N64 games.
